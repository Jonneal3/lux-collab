# Active Markets

Operating rule
- Lux should treat neighborhoods, not whole metros, as the real unit of market selection.

Priority operating markets now

## West Palm Beach, FL
- Focus neighborhoods: El Cid, Flamingo Park, South End / SoSo, Old Northwood
- Why it matters: strong teardown and historic-sensitive redevelopment behavior, desirable product, and clear builder specialization
- Canonical note: [[West Palm Beach - Market Playbook]]

## Tampa, FL
- Focus neighborhoods: Davis Islands, Beach Park, Palma Ceia, Sunset Park
- Why it matters: strong infill luxury pockets, lot scarcity in the best areas, and clear builder demand
- Canonical note: [[Tampa - Market Playbook]]

## Charlotte, NC
- Focus neighborhoods: Eastover, Dilworth, Foxcroft, Sedgefield, Myers Park, Cotswold, SouthPark
- Why it matters: established luxury infill neighborhoods with a broader builder ecosystem than smaller boutique markets
- Canonical note: [[Charlotte - Market Playbook]]

## Atlanta, GA
- Focus neighborhoods: Buckhead, Tuxedo Park, Chastain
- Why it matters: high-end infill demand, deep buyer capital, and strong luxury developer activity
- Canonical note: [[Atlanta - Market Playbook]]

## Austin, TX
- Focus neighborhoods: Westlake Hills, Tarrytown, Rollingwood, Barton Hills / Barton Creek, Old Enfield / Pemberton Heights, Lake Austin Waterfront
- Why it matters: very strong luxury pricing and highly constrained premium dirt in the best pockets
- Canonical note: [[Austin - Market Playbook]]

## Scottsdale, AZ
- Focus neighborhoods: Paradise Valley, Silverleaf / DC Ranch, Arcadia, Troon / Desert Mountain
- Why it matters: concentrated luxury demand with clear buyer and builder appetite
- Canonical note: [[Scottsdale - Market Playbook]]

Entry rules

- Build buyer coverage before scaling seller outreach

- Confirm real pricing spread, not just theoretical upside
- Keep a market narrow until quote confidence is dependable
