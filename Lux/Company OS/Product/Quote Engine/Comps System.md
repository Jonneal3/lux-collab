# Comps System

What comps are
- Reusable sold listings for teardown, land, or closely related infill pricing reference.
- Org-scoped comp records that can be reused across properties and pricing workflows.

Where they come from
- Zillow, Redfin, MLS, or manual research
- Internal observations from real deal work
- On-the-fly comp entry from pricing workflows

Preferred comp profile
- Teardown lots and vacant land first
- Older housing stock where teardown is plausible
- Recent sold comps when possible
- Clear explanation for why the comp belongs in the set

How they should be used
- Seed system buy boxes when direct buyer quote data is weak
- Support quote triangulation on specific properties
- Inform market and neighborhood intelligence
- Attach to specific properties during estimate review
- Stay reusable instead of being trapped inside one quote flow

How they should not be used
- They should not overwrite strong buyer-specific behavior blindly
- They should not be treated as equally reliable without quality filters
- They should not directly rewrite buyer-generated buy boxes
- They should not drive gradient behavior the same way direct quotes do

Quality rules to improve
- Separate teardown from renovation better
- Prefer recent sales where possible
- Keep clear notes on why a comp is relevant
- Use listing text, images, and other heuristics to detect noisy comps

Canonical system behavior retained from legacy drafts
- Use comps to seed system-generated buy boxes when quote coverage is thin
- Let operators attach 2 to 3 nearby comps during estimate review
- Keep comp influence as a separate input rather than pretending it is the same as direct buyer quote behavior
